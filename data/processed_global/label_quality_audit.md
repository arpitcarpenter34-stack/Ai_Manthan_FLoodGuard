## 1. Executive Summary & Audit Mandate

In standard machine learning workflows, binary flood classification labels are frequently generated by applying arbitrary thresholds or treating uncataloged time periods as negative ("flood-free") examples. 

Under **Task 2.1**, a strict scientific audit of `label_generation.py` was conducted to enforce physical validity:
1. **Separation of Statistical Flow Anomalies from Official Flood Stage**: Flow percentiles (such as $Q_{99}$) reflect statistical rarity within a historical sample, not necessarily overbank inundation. They are now explicitly renamed as `label_statistical_high_flow` unless supported by an official published flood datum.
2. **Official Flood Stage Verification**: Verified published flood-stage datums from the NOAA / NWS National Water Prediction Service (NWPS) are now integrated for gauged locations (`label_official_flood_observed`).
3. **Bankfull Datum Provenance**: Bankfull elevation thresholds were audited for all 9 stations, distinguishing official action stages from empirical geomorphic Leopold-Wolman estimates.
4. **Estimated vs. Measured Inundation Depth**: Inundation depth computed as $(h(t) - h_{\text{bankfull}})$ is explicitly termed **estimated inundation depth** (`label_estimated_inundation_depth_m`) and never misrepresented as direct physical sensor measurement.
5. **Observation Uncertainty & Non-Reporting Distinction**: Unobserved periods are strictly maintained as `NaN` with boolean masks. The absence of a NOAA storm event report is explicitly tagged as `NO_OFFICIAL_REPORT_FILED`, preventing false negative assumptions.

## 2. Statistical Flow Anomalies vs. Official Flood Stages

```
                ▲ River Stage / Streamflow
                │
                │   [Major Flood Stage]   ────── NWS Major Datum (e.g. 5.18 m at Little Falls)
                │   [Moderate Flood]      ────── NWS Moderate Datum (4.27 m)
                │   [Minor Flood Stage]   ────── NWS Official Flood Stage (3.66 m) ==> label_official_flood_observed = 1.0
                │   [Action Stage]        ────── Bankfull Threshold (3.05 m)      ==> Inundation Depth > 0.0 m
                │
── ── ── ── ── ─┼─ ─[Q99 Flow Anomaly]─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ 99th Empirical Percentile   ==> label_statistical_high_flow = 1.0
                │   (Statistical Anomaly)         (Historical 1% frequency)
                │
                │   [Median Baseflow]     ────── Normal Dry Weather Flow (0.5 - 1.5 m)
                └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────► Time (Hours)
```

### The Physical Flaw of Conflating $Q_{99}$ with Flooding:
- In heavily incised canyons or deep bedrock gorges (such as portions of the Upper Potomac or mountain streams), a 99th-percentile flow discharge may remain entirely contained within channel walls without overtopping banks.
- Conversely, in low-gradient alluvial floodplains or urban drainage sumps, minor high flows compounded by culvert blockages or backwater surges can cause severe street inundation well below $Q_{99}$.

### Disambiguated Implementation in `label_generation.py`:
- **`label_statistical_high_flow`**:
  $$\text{label}_{\text{high\_flow}}(t) = \begin{cases} 1.0 & \text{if } Q(t) \ge Q_{99} \text{ or } h(t) \ge h_{99} \\ 0.0 & \text{if measured and } Q(t) < Q_{99} \\ \text{NaN} & \text{if } Q(t) \text{ is unmeasured} \end{cases}$$
- **`label_official_flood_observed`**:
  $$\text{label}_{\text{official\_flood}}(t) = \begin{cases} 1.0 & \text{if } h(t) \ge h_{\text{NWS\_minor\_flood}} \\ 0.0 & \text{if measured and } h(t) < h_{\text{NWS\_minor\_flood}} \\ \text{NaN} & \text{if stage unmeasured or station lacks official NWS datum} \end{cases}$$

## 4. Inundation Depth Estimation Formulation

Inundation depth is a critical parameter for PS-11 hydraulic risk mapping. However, river gauges measure 1D water elevation above a local gauge datum, **not** 2D overland water depth across streets or floodplains.

### Strict Mathematical Definition:
$$\text{estimated\_inundation\_depth\_m}(t) = \begin{cases} \max\left(0.0, h(t) - h_{\text{bankfull}}\right) & \text{if } h(t) \text{ is validly measured} \\ \text{NaN} & \text{if } h(t) \text{ is missing / unobserved} \end{cases}$$

### Scientific Rules Enforced:
1. When water level $h(t) \le h_{\text{bankfull}}$, the depth is $0.0\text{ m}$ (water is fully confined within channel banks).
2. When water level $h(t) > h_{\text{bankfull}}$, depth represents estimated average water column height above the river bank lip.
3. If $h(t)$ is `NaN`, depth is strictly `NaN` with `label_estimated_inundation_depth_mask = False`. **Under no circumstances is missing stage converted to 0.0 m.**
4. In all reports, code, and APIs, this variable is named `label_estimated_inundation_depth_m`. Describing this as "measured depth" is scientifically inaccurate and prohibited.

## 5. NOAA Storm Events Spatial & Temporal Matching Audit

NOAA NCEI Storm Events database records post-disaster incident evaluations compiled by Weather Forecast Offices (WFO). 

### Spatial & Temporal Matching Criteria:
- **Spatial Buffer:** $\le 35\text{ km}$ for urban/tributary gauges; $\le 50\text{ km}$ for large regional river reaches.
- **Bounding Box Pre-Filter:**
  $$\text{lat} \in \left[\text{lat}_{\text{gauge}} \pm \frac{R}{111}\right], \quad \text{lon} \in \left[\text{lon}_{\text{gauge}} \pm \frac{R}{111 \cos(\text{lat})}\right]$$
- **Great-Circle Distance Verification:** Exact distance verified via Haversine formula.
- **Temporal Intersection:** Gauge timestep $t \in [t_{\text{begin}}, t_{\text{end}}]$.

### Findings & Uncertainty Handling:
- NOAA event records typically carry a spatial footprint of an entire county or municipality.
- In `noaa_storm_events_2023_floods.csv`, 6,684 events were matched.
- When an active NOAA event intersects a gauge buffer, `label_noaa_reported = 1` with `noaa_reporting_confidence = "CONFIRMED_NWS_REPORTED_EVENT"`.
- When no NOAA event is filed during an hour, `label_noaa_reported = 0` with `noaa_reporting_confidence = "NO_OFFICIAL_REPORT_FILED"`.
- **Audit Conclusion:** The absence of a filed NOAA emergency report does **not** prove zero overland flooding occurred (e.g. localized urban street ponding or basement seepage often goes unfiled by emergency managers). Training heads must treat NOAA labels as positive-only supervision indicators.
